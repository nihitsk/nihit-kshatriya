Books

Books that I have thoroughly enjoyed reading and that have shaped how I understand the world around me.

Shoe Dog
Phil Knight
Fascinating story of pace, scale and ambition behind building Nike. As you read, you can't miss the undercurrent of constant hustle even when the company scaled heights.
The Key Man
Simon Clark & Will Louch
It made me believe in the power of delusion to scale heights and also how the same delusion can make you fall hard. Unputdownable.
The Money Trap
Alok Sama
Truly gripping storytelling and a peek into the world of Masa (Softbank). Unreal to see billion-dollar decisions made on instinct and $5,000 bottles of La Tâche.
More to come
One Up On Wall Street
Peter Lynch
Lynch taught me that I can probably spot a good business if I observe carefully in my daily life. Changed what I look at when I visit a store or a shopping mall.
Money Wise
Deepak Shenoy
Reads as lucid as Deepak talks, so you'll finish it in one go. The idea that stuck with me: get rich at your day job, markets are for staying ahead of inflation. I do remind him about it in appraisal season ;)
Flash Crash
Liam Vaughan
A man trading from his parents' bedroom crashed the market for a micro-second and sent ripples across the world. Made a killing and then lost most of his own money to garden-variety con men.
Flash Boys
Michael Lewis
Reads like a Netflix thriller. Someone drilled a cable through land & mountains to save milliseconds on a trade order. A true story and made me realize how the real edge in trading is technology (not information).
Outlive
Peter Attia
Attia makes a hard-to-argue case for exercising right now for the body you want when you turn eighty. I need to start doing enough on the fitness part. Staring at stock tickers never made anyone healthier.
Never Split the Difference
Chris Voss
I love this book because I agree to all the tactics and psychological hacks it teaches. But, I have never been able to follow any one of them. Behaviour is hormonal, can't be trained beyond a point.
Freakonomics
Steven D. Levitt & Stephen J. Dubner
Taught me to ask the right questions, develop second order thinking and spot unintended consequences. Whenever I read it again, I realize something new.
Fooled by Randomness
Nassim Nicholas Taleb
Taleb makes you suspicious of everything, including yourself. Helps you embrace uncertainty or is basically saying "sab likha hua hai".
